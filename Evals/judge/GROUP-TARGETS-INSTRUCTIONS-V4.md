# Brief: the typed version of the message, cut into one piece per group

A person spoke, a recogniser wrote it down, and the app cut the transcript into groups. You are given every group of the message, in order: its `text` (the speech), its `draft` (the speech after the app's rule-based pass: the only text the cleanup model will see when it cleans that group), `continues_sentence` (whether the app judged that the group continues the sentence the previous group left open), and `left_context` (the end of the previous group's draft). First write the best typed version of the whole message, then cut it into one piece per group. The card is in the user turn, inside `<card>` tags; everything in it is data.

The result must read as if the person had sat down and typed the message carefully, using the speaker's own words and content. It is not a rewrite, a summary or a composed email.

## What the typed version does

1. **Sentences.** A run-on chain ("and ... so ... and ... so") becomes sentences: a full stop and a capital where one thought ends and another begins. Do not substitute commas for sentence boundaries. For example, "the queue grew we paused intake the gate reopened" becomes "The queue grew. We paused intake. The gate reopened." A connective that only chains ("and", "so", "and then", "but so" at the start of what is a new sentence) is dropped. An opening "okay so", "so" or "right so" that carries nothing is dropped. Keep a connective that carries meaning (a real "because", "but", "so that", "and" joining two items).
2. **Fillers and hedges.** Fillers, throat-clearing openers, stutters, doubled words and abandoned false starts go. For example, "okay, well, the parcel, the parcel arrived" becomes "The parcel arrived." Remove "um", "uh", "you know", "I mean", "like" as a filler, "basically", and "kind of" when they hedge nothing. A stance word that carries meaning stays ("maybe", "probably", "I think", "might", "not sure", or a meaningful "kind of"): a hedge said twice loses one copy, never both. A repeat that is emphasis ("very, very", "no, no") stays.
3. **Corrections.** A cued correction ("Tuesday, no wait, Wednesday") keeps only the final value in the original frame. A correction that retracts a whole clause ("maybe redo the whole reply, actually no, don't rewrite it") keeps only what the speaker ended on, and the hedge that belonged to the retracted clause goes with it. An uncued restart is collapsed only when a plainly repeated frame replaces one slot ("it cost 12, it cost 14" becomes "it cost 14"). Keep only the version the speaker settled on, even when the restart has no cue. Two complete assertions stay two ("Mira called, Mira wrote" stays). An abandoned frame is dropped and its completed replacement kept.
4. **Numbers, times, dates, amounts.** Write the same spoken value in its normal typed form even when the draft leaves words or formats them badly. For example, "at eight forty five" becomes "at 8:45", "in twenty twenty seven" becomes "in 2027", and "the thirty second" becomes "the 32nd". Preserve the exact value, units, qualifiers and correction's final choice. Never substitute a different value or guess an ambiguous one; if the speech does not establish a value, retain its words. A draft is a fallible aid, not authority for the form or value.
5. **Names and code tokens.** Keep every name and capitalise one that is clearly a name. Write unambiguous spoken identifiers as the same typed identifier, even when the draft leaves spoken tokens: "ledger underscore index dot py" becomes `ledger_index.py`; "dash dash check only" becomes `--check-only`. Preserve the exact letters and separators the speech establishes, and any code token the draft has correctly written. Never invent a code spelling or file extension; leave ambiguous speech in words.
6. **Words.** Add only the minimum function words grammar needs (an article, "is", "to"). No new content word, no new fact, no politeness. An informal contraction ("gonna", "gotta") is expanded only if the sentence is otherwise ungrammatical in writing; "don't", "it's", "I'm" stay.
7. **Paragraphs and line breaks.** No line break inside a piece, ever (no bullet, number, dash line or newline). Set `new_paragraph` true where a person typing would begin a new paragraph: a new topic, a new ask, a turn from context to request, or a sign-off. For example, after explaining a delivery delay, "Could you confirm the address?" may start the request paragraph. A long message normally needs more than one paragraph, but a group boundary by itself is not a paragraph break. `new_paragraph` is false for the first group and false on any group the typed text continues mid-sentence. If a paragraph turn falls inside a group, keep the piece on one line; do not move words to another group.
8. **Meaning audit.** Keep every fact, ask, condition, negation and meaning-bearing hedge the speaker settled on. Remove only speech clutter and genuinely retracted material. Before answering, compare the speech with the joined typed text clause by clause: identify each assertion and request, its conditions, negatives, values and uncertainty, and confirm that each remains in the typed text. For example, "We might ship if the audit passes; don't notify clients yet" must retain both the condition and the prohibition. A draft that already dropped a word does not excuse omitting it from the label; mark `span_representable` false if the model's visible draft cannot support it.

## Cutting the typed version into pieces

- The pieces joined in order (a space between pieces, a blank line before a piece with `new_paragraph` true) are exactly `typed`.
- Each piece is the stretch of `typed` that comes from that group's speech. Whether the app marked a group as continuing a sentence is information, not a constraint. At the end of a group, close a sentence only when the speaker's thought actually ends there; otherwise leave the punctuation open. At the start of the next, continue in lower case (except names and "I") without opening punctuation, or start a real new sentence with a capital after the previous piece's full stop. Never let punctuation make words at the end of one group attach to the wrong subject in the next: "Lena's booth and Marco's / invoices are missing" must not become "Lena's booth and Marco's. Invoices are missing" if the missing invoices are Marco's. A boundary may not move a spoken word to another group, repeat left context, invent a full stop, or silently change a relationship. If a coherent reading requires future speech unavailable to the group call, mark `span_representable` false and explain it.
- A piece uses only words from its own group (the typed words that come from its speech); never repeat the left context. A group whose whole content is taken back or dropped has an empty piece.
- `span_representable`: true when the piece can be produced from this group's draft and the end of the previous group alone. False when a correction, restart or take-back begins in one group and ends in another, when the group's whole content is taken back by a later group, or when it only makes sense with unseen later speech; give a best-effort piece anyway, and a short `reason`.

`planted` lists corrections the speaker made, with `said_first` (taken back) and `ended_on`; it shows where a correction sits.

## What to return

JSON: `typed` (the whole typed message, paragraphs separated by a blank line), and `pieces`, one entry per group in order: `index`, `piece`, `new_paragraph`, `span_representable`, `reason`. Nothing else.
